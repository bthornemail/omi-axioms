## Abstract

The Fano lines are the Hamming code, and it tiles the ASCII table.

## Statement

The check holds when each of the following holds, in this order (taken from its assertions):


**The lines, their complements and the two constants: 16 codewords**

1. 16 codewords
2. weights: 0, seven lines of 3, seven complements of 4, 7

**A linear code, distance 3: the Hamming (7,4) code**

3. every two codewords at least 3 bits apart: one flipped bit is corrected
4. 4 data bits: 2^4 codewords in 7 bits

**Perfect over the ASCII table: 16 balls of 8 = 128**

5. ASCII 0x${x.toString(16)} is within one bit of exactly one codeword
6. 16 balls of 8: 1 codeword and its 7 one-bit neighbours

**The codewords as characters**

7. NUL, BEL, ^Y, RS, *, -, 3, 4, K, L, R, U, a, f, x, DEL
8. BEL is the line 1-3-2, '*' the line 2-6-4
9. none in the separator gap 0x3A-0x40

## Notes

- What it checks: the seven Fano lines of the 16-index, their complements, 0 and 0x7F are 16 words closed under XOR at minimum distance 3 (the Hamming (7,4) code); every one of the 128 ASCII codes is within one bit of exactly one of them (16 balls of 8); which characters they are; that none falls in the separator gap 0x3A-0x40 (a claim from the conversation, which fails).
- What it does not check: the zipper itself (I-107) or the compare-and-exchange (I-177, I-178), which the author names alongside.
