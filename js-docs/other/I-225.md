## Abstract

22 55 — the 2 and the 5 of the base, eleven times, added without a carry.

## Statement

The check holds when each of the following holds, in this order (taken from its assertions):


**.5 → 5 → 25 → 225**

1. the half, in base 10 = 2 x 5, is 5 (I-219)
2. `assert.equal(5 * 5, 25);`
3. 25 nine times in the 225
4. `assert.equal(225, (3 * 5) ** 2);`

**F2: F squared, with and without the carries**

5. F squared with carries: 225, this record's number
6. F squared in GF(2), without carries: 0x55, the boot mark's half (I-222)
7. the carries are the 140 between them
8. and 5 squared without carries is 0x11

**22 55: the 2 and the 5, eleven times, in both bases**

9. decimal: 2 and 5 times eleven
10. hexadecimal: 2 and 5 times 0x11, which is 5 squared without carries
11. 2 and 5: the two primes of the base
12. 0x22: the quote, Shift + 2 on the teletype (I-218), the Catch-22 byte
13. 0x55: U

**Added without a carry: the sum is the XOR**

14. they share no bit
15. `assert.equal(0x22 ^ 0x55, 0x77); assert.equal(0x22 | 0x55, 0x77);`
16. so adding them carries nothing: the sum, the XOR and the OR agree, 0x77
17. and in decimal too: 22 + 55 = 77
18. their XNOR: 0x88

**2F 25: the escaped slash**

19. %
20. a URL writes an escaped slash as %2F: the escape character 0x25 carrying 2F
21. /

**0x2255 under the delta law**

22. the word 2255: period 4 under 0x1D1D
23. and 8 under 0x1D (I-195)

**BCD: the hex digits read as decimal**

24. 0x25 + 0x25 in binary: 4A, not a decimal
25. with the adjust: 50, as 25 + 25
26. the adjust is the 6 between the bases
27. and I-219's c, 0x25 - 25 = 12, is that 6 for the 2 in the tens place
28. 22 + 55 needs no adjust: 0x77 is already the decimal 77
29. each digit pair sums to 7: no nibble passes 9
30. F is no BCD digit: F squared, 225 with carries and 0x55 without, lies outside BCD
