## Abstract

The Fano lottery: 14 tickets, and two of any three rebuild the third.

## Statement

The check holds when each of the following holds, in this order (taken from its assertions):


**Every draw of three from 1-14 meets a ticket in two, and the two rebuild the ticket**

1. ${numbers} numbers
2. ${tickets} tickets: the lines of two PG(${k - 1}, 2)
3. draw {${draw}} meets a ticket in two numbers
4. {${draw}}: ${p} and ${q} rebuild ${third} by XOR
5. all ${draws} draws

**Why: two of any three share a plane; any two points share exactly one line**

6. ${a}, ${b}: ${same ? "one line" : "no line"}
7. 14 tickets: the 7 lines of each plane

## Notes

- What it checks: every draw of three from 1-14 (364), 1-30 (4,060) and 1-62 (37,820) meets one of the tickets (the lines of two copies of PG(k-1, 2): 14, 70, 310) in two numbers, and those two rebuild the ticket's third by XOR; any two numbers of one plane share exactly one ticket, of two planes none.
- What it does not check: that 14 tickets is the fewest that guarantee a match of two (MathWorld gives the construction, not the bound here), or the author's 16^4 and 16^10.
