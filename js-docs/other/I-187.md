## Abstract

The tripods of the 2^n-ions: e_a e_b = ±e_(a ^ b), and the triples are the lines of PG(n-1, 2).

## Statement

The check holds when each of the following holds, in this order (taken from its assertions):


**A product of units is ± the unit at the XOR of their indices**

1. dimension ${n}: e${a} e${b} = ±e${a ^ b}

**So the tripods are the lines of PG(n-1, 2): (2^n - 1)(2^n - 2)/6**

2. octonions 7, sedenions 35, trigintaduonions 155, 64-ions 651
3. the 64-ions have 63 imaginary units: the 6-bit closure

**Wikipedia's 155, as listed**

4. the listed 155 are exactly the lines of PG(4, 2)
5. e${a} e${b} = ±e${c} in the trigintaduonions

## Notes

- What it checks: in the Cayley-Dickson doubling, the product of two units is ± the unit at the XOR of their indices (every pair in dimensions 8, 16, 32; in 64, every unit with every fifth partner); the triples a ^ b ^ c = 0 number (2^n - 1)(2^n - 2)/6: 7, 35, 155, 651; Wikipedia's 155 trigintaduonion triples, as listed, are exactly the lines of PG(4, 2), and each closes under the product.
- What it does not check: the signs (which units multiply to +e_c and which to -e_c), the five types Wikipedia sorts the triples into, or the tripod-packing problem.
