## Abstract

Hashing and encryption compared — one way, both ways, and the slide rule inside.

## Statement

The check holds when each of the following holds, in this order (taken from its assertions):


**Hashes: one way, a fixed function of the literal**

1. SHA-1, git's name for it (I-220)
2. SHAKE256
3. always the same: no key, no secret

**Checksums: the same idea, without the one-way**

4. CRC-32: a remainder over GF(2)
5. FNV-1a: XOR, then multiply
6. the XOR fold of all 639 bytes: 0x5C, one byte

**The delta law as a fold**

7. 320 pairs
8. from 0 and from 0x4242: c684 and 84c6, a byte palindrome
9. the seed difference 0x4242 returns every 4 steps of the linear part
10. and 320 is a multiple of 4: the difference comes back whole (structure)
11. the mirror needed c6 XOR 84 = 0x42 too: 1 in 256 (chance)

**Ciphers: both ways, with the key**

12. AES-256-CTR, key 0
13. one bit of key, another ciphertext
14. decrypted with the key: the literal back
15. the XOR one-time pad
16. literal XOR pad XOR pad = literal: the doctrine's own operation is the cipher

**The slide rule: multiplying by adding positions**

17. generator 3 reaches every nonzero byte once
18. `assert.equal(log8[0xde], 211); assert.equal(log8[0xad], 118);`
19. de times ad: slide 211 + 118 = 74 (mod 255), read f1
20. `assert.equal(exp8[(211 + 118) % 255], 0xf1);`
21. the inverse of de, 44 ticks round: 90, AES's S-box begins here

**A barcode's error correction is the slide rule**

22. FIG. 7B: 56 bits
23. FIG. 7C: the data blocks
24. FIG. 7D: the patent's six check blocks, reproduced by the slide rule
