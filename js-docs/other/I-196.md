## Abstract

The decades: braille's dots, EBCDIC's zones, ASCII's columns.

## Statement

The check holds when each of the following holds, in this order (taken from its assertions):


**Braille: four decades on one base of ten**

1. a-j use only the upper four dots (1, 2, 4, 5: bits 0, 1, 3, 4)
2. each decade adds one fixed pattern: dot 3 (0x04), dots 3 and 6 (0x24), dot 6 (0x20)
3. adding, not changing: every decade keeps the base's dots

**Dot 6 is ASCII's case bit**

4. dot 6 alone is 0x20

**Octal precision: 8 columns of 32**

5. the top three bits are one octal digit
6. ASCII: BEL in control, '0' in punctuation and digits, 'A' in capitals, 'a' in lowercase
7. in octal 'A' is 101: column 1 of 64, place 01 (the octal digits are 2, 3, 3 bits)

**EBCDIC's zones: three decades of letters**

8. zones C, D, E: three decades
9. A-I and J-R nine each, S-Z eight (starting at 2)

## Notes

- What it checks: the braille decades (Wikipedia's cells for a-j, k-t, u-ù, â-w) add fixed patterns 0x00, 0x04, 0x24, 0x20 in the Unicode braille block; dot 6 is 0x20, ASCII's case bit; a byte's top three bits are one octal digit, 8 columns of 32; EBCDIC (code page 037, from iconv) has letters in zones C, D, E.
- The EBCDIC codes were read from iconv -t IBM037 and are written into the record as literals.
