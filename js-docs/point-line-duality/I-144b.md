## Abstract

Base-36 as a literal /A-Z0-9/ block.

## Statement

The check holds when each of the following holds, in this order (taken from its assertions):


**The literal block: uppercase only**

1. 36 symbols
2. '${c}' is in /A-Z0-9/
3. '${c}' is not lowercase

**Direct indexing (no conversion functions)**

4. 0 -> '0'
5. 9 -> '9'
6. 10 -> 'A'
7. 19 -> 'J'
8. 23 -> 'N'
9. 24 -> 'O'
10. 27 -> 'R'
11. 31 -> 'V'
12. 35 -> 'Z'
13. '0' -> 0
14. '9' -> 9
15. 'A' -> 10
16. 'J' -> 19
17. 'V' -> 31
18. 'Z' -> 35

**The block IS the base-36 alphabet**

19. 0
20. 9
21. 10 -> A
22. 31 -> V
23. 35 -> Z
24. 36 -> 10
25. 255 -> 73
26. round-trip ${v}

**The block contains the 36-block of ASCII**

27. 0x${c.toString(16)} in block
28. 0x${c.toString(16)} in block
29. 10 digits + 26 letters = 36

**The gap in ASCII between digits and letters**

30. digit gap starts at 0x3A
31. letter gap ends at 0x41
32. 7-character gap
33. gap = :;<=>?@

**The base-36 sub-context ignores prefix syntax**

34. ${src} (${val}) -> "V"

**The base-36 block as a self-delineating alphabet**

35. '${c}' (0x${code.toString(16)}) in /A-Z0-9/

**The contrast: what the pre-encoded version gets wrong**

36. toString(36) gives lowercase 'v'
37. the literal block gives uppercase 'V'
38. toString(36) ≠ the literal block (case difference)
39. uppercased toString(36) = the block
